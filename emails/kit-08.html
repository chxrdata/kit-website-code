Miami, FL Garden of Hopes and Dreams

Hello everyone! Happy now-it’s-July, I hope you're all doing well and creating new stories. As for me, well...this is the story of how I became a hand model.

I may know what you’re thinking at the moment.

You: …What?

Me: Yes.

You: Don’t even joke, lad.

After graduation, I took two weeks to relax before packing most of my bags and driving down to Miami! My internship with Carnival Cruise Line kicked off on May 18th; the first day ended with a 3-hour-long welcome dinner that induced delirium in every intern by the time things wrapped up (at long last) at 9pm.

While the first week was mostly onboarding and getting familiar with Windows OS, I hit the ground running and began working on design projects pretty quickly. The creative team at Carnival comprises 17 people, so it’s been really fun to get to know everyone and learn about each designer’s specialties and work experience (Bari Elliot, who was a UFGD senior when we were juniors, is also at Carnival and I’ve enjoyed shadowing/getting to know her and her work better too).

In addition to a bunch of projects (ranging from a logo for a new ship restaurant to digital ads and billboards for Carnival’s partnership with the NY Jets), I’ve had the opportunity to do a handful of fun intern excursions: touring a ship, volunteering with Feeding South Florida, and eating a surprising amount of company-sponsored ice cream!

A few weeks into the internship, an opportunity came up to attend a content photoshoot aboard Carnival Celebration, one of the biggest ships in Carnival’s fleet. I woke up at 6am on a Sunday and drove out to the Port of Miami for a day of what I thought would be minor behind-the-scenes work moving lights and taking notes. What I didn’t expect was that the shoot would fall over an hour behind schedule, resulting in a rush to film every required scene before the ship departed the port at 2pm.

The first scene involved shots in which a passenger hands a rewards card to a sales clerk, no face or acting nuance required. With the models still getting camera-ready and the clock ticking, one of the creative managers looked around, saw my hands were groomed, and asked me to model on the spot! AAAAAAAAAAA. It was a little stressful at first, but the photoshoot crew were super kind and gave very clear instructions on how to position myself and my hand. I probably repeated the same action about 50 times. Being a surprise hand model was a really cool experience and something I didn’t think I’d ever be doing. Weird times!


On the personal side, I’ve dedicated the past couple months to taking care of myself and creating new routines now that college is over! I’m still reading a bunch (I have new fiction recommendations if anyone is looking for a book :D) and searching for new events/social activities to get out of the house more often. Deltarune Chapter 5 has swallowed up my life this past week lmao. I also have high hopes for rest and whimsy this upcoming weekend; I spent the last two weekends driving up and down from Gainesville to fully pack the furniture and move out of my apartment, which involved a lot of physical labor fueled by cookies from Gideon’s Bakehouse in Orlando (highly recommend).

Reading about some of your guys’ moving processes in the previous newsletters has gotten me emotional, and now I’ve felt it too. But even though the physical stuff is boxed away and we’ve scattered to the four winds, Gainesville was our home for four years, and in memory, it is even now! If I close my eyes, I can so easily transport myself back to the Stu. The late-night work sessions, walking breaks during David’s class, and endless hijinks with all of you are still so near to my heart. A part of us is still there.
