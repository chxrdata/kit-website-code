**Please welcome Guest Writer Eury Kim!**

When I first moved to NYC, the economy was just picking itself back up after the 2008 recession. Millennials were being labeled as the ME ME ME generation, the most self-obsessed group of youngsters to date. K-beauty was just starting to get a lot of traction, and Big Bang, 2NE1, and G-Dragon were at the top of the K-pop game. Marketing hit peak millennial, and everything was friendly, round, and pastel.

Not everyone was on Instagram yet; it only allowed for square-format, single-image uploads. Influencer media was at its starting point, and the word influencer itself was pretty new. The NYC it-girls were paving their way via their fashion blogs: Jenna Lyons at J.Crew was a highlight, Greta Gerwig was becoming an indie-film icon, and Emily Weiss at Glossier defined cool.

Emily was famous for being an intern in the fashion/beauty world whose blog, Into the Gloss, had really taken off. She then made her way up to being CEO of a $1.8 billion company. Glossier was the quintessential millennial beauty brand that was skincare first; their products came in millennial pink packaging, typeset in Aperçu. Everything came with a pack of free stickers, because nostalgia is always a thing, and the ’90s were back to redefine mainstream millennial culture.

At the time, I made about $30K in NYC and had basically nothing left over at the end of the month after paying rent. But when Glossier launched their Balm Dotcom (BDC), I was obsessed. I saved up to place an order. They were perfectly squishy and came with a pink bubble-wrap pouch. And I wasn't the only one obsessed. BDC did pretty well on the market, so much so that Glossier came out with like 20+ more flavors.

Fast-forward about a decade: I had moved away for grad school, then moved back into an area of Brooklyn on the verge of gentrification. By then, Gen Z had started to pick up where millennials had left off as the most self-obsessed generation. As we passed on the torch, mainstream culture moved on too and Y2K trends came back. Ariana Grande, Olivia Rodrigo, and Jenna Ortega became the new it-girls. Jenna Lyons and Greta Gerwig had gone on to do bigger and greater things. Emily Weiss stepped down as CEO of Glossier, and the brand had, in the meantime, expanded their line to makeup but was “still, skincare first.”

My Balm Dotcom obsession waned a bit. Until last year, when they came out with their Magnolia Bakery Banana Pudding flavor. I had just moved to Florida and it had been a full two years since I left NYC. I missed Banana Pudding, but I also missed the city, its density, its culture, and its energy that no other place on Earth has yet to replicate. So with a new Banana Pudding BDC in hand, I went to class and got to know you guys better. I hated the sweaty stickiness and the chaos of hurricane season. But you taught me to embrace the quirks that come with living in the subtropics of this peninsula. Your presence helped me get through adjusting to Florida, for two years.

Recently, I was walking to school and realized that I'm okay with the sweaty, sticky me; I learned to look forward to cooling off in my office. And I'm not too bothered by hurricane season or football traffic as much; I know how to wait until it passes through. Overall, I feel less weary of having left somewhere I called home for a long time. So when I bought Glossier's hyped up summer special-edition apple-flavored I ❤️ NY Balm Dotcom, I thought it would make me feel like the Banana Pudding flavor had. Instead, I found it overwhelming and too apple-juicy for me. I returned it.

I still ❤️ NY but I said goodbye to the millennial me. I’m not Glossier’s target audience anymore and I'm happy with the memories I have of NYC without a matching BDC. Banana Pudding Glossier didn’t make it feel okay to spend the last of my thirties in Gainesville. I have you to miss, and that’s a treat, because I also get to look forward to hearing that you're back in Gainesville to catch up. In fact, I’m not really saying goodbye to the millennial me, but I'm leaving behind the millennial that I was: pastel, weary, and restless.

I like to think that I’m carrying memories forward instead, like The Giver. I'm also in love with the idea that I'm passing things on, including our time together. I'll be here for a bit, so call me up if you're back. But if we ever find each other at opposite ends of the world, don't hesitate to email and say hi. I’ll be here, like you were for me.
