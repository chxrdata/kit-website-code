Coral Springs, FL Every Dawn's a Mountain

Hi everyone! I want to start off by saying that I miss you all dearly and have enjoyed reading each week’s newsletter to see what everyone’s been up to (and huge thanks to Del for arranging this)! I didn’t get a chance to say goodbye to most of you before I had to leave town following graduation, but just know that I think of you all often :)

Anyway, I initially signed up to write this week’s newsletter because it would be the week after my return from my highly anticipated family trip to Ireland. I am writing this now, still having never visited “The Motherland.”

My dad was recovering from an emergency eye surgery he had back in mid-April, which involved a gas bubble placed inside his eye to keep pressure while healing. He was told by the doctor that he’d be good to go in time for our trip, but the bubble would need 6 weeks to fully dissolve before he’d be allowed to fly or else it would rapidly expand with altitude (a scenario to avoid at all costs). Our scheduled flight was exactly 6 weeks away from his surgery, so it was cutting it close.

We’d check in with my dad every day to ask how small the bubble looked and it seemed to be dissolving but at a slower pace than anticipated. Our chance of travel was also slowly waning. We held out hope for as long as possible and even packed our bags just in case. The morning of our flight, my dad woke up with the bubble still visible in his eye. We had to make the tough last-minute call to reschedule our whole trip to NEXT summer. And I had to unpack all my bags :(

This was a pretty disheartening experience, especially when our Ireland trip was the one thing my family was looking forward to after a pretty rough start to 2026. I feel like every time I’ve tried to pick myself up this year, I’ve been pushed down again, but I guess that’s life and I can’t let it get me down too long.

Turning to better news, my family was able to go on a backup trip to a small town in the mountains: Hendersonville, NC. It wasn’t Ireland, but it was a welcome change of pace and scenery. I was happily mesmerized by our mountain top view: perfect for watching sunsets flood the sky in color and thunderstorms roll shadows over the rural landscape. The slower pace invited me to watch birds (I get it now, Oskar), butterflies, bunnies, fireflies, and woodchucks (or “chunks,” as my siblings and I lovingly call them). We also had some unwelcome guests in the cabin: spiders of all sizes, crickets, and a bee.

Some other activities I enjoyed throughout my vacation week:

  • Exploring a 3-story antique store
  • Going on a hike and cooling off in the mist of huge waterfalls
  • Roasting s’mores
  • Picking lavender and sunflowers at a local farm
  • Staging a haunted candlestick photo shoot with my siblings

On my drive back from NC, we stopped in Gainesville with a U-Haul to move me out of my house, which was kind of traumatic, but I’m trying not to think about it too much. When I got to my Gainesville house, every surface in every room was covered in dead lovebugs. After vacuuming up all the bug corpses, I went on one last long walk around campus, passed FAC, tried to use my ID to get in out of curiosity, got the red light, cried and continued walking. In 23 hours I was in and out of Gainesville, and the only bedroom I’ve ever had to myself was left empty and behind.

I’m realizing that got sad again so I’ll try to end on a happy note: One of our last days in Hendersonville, we got dinner at a local Irish pub followed by homemade ice cream from the Celtic Creamery. Between bites of shepherd's pie, I joked, “It’s like we made it to Ireland after all! The only thing missing is the sheep.”

6/18/26 Update:

I wrote the above entry in a thunderstorm on the highway somewhere between Gainesville and Coral Springs as the car map ETA read 12:08am. I successfully made it back home to SoFlo on Monday and have spent the past 3 days cleaning out my garage and bedroom to unload the U-Haul of stuff I brought home, but things are still pretty messy around here.

Some final good news: I received a contract for a full-time graphic design and marketing position that I plan to sign.

Another plot twist: I sprained my ankle last night while playing basketball with friends.

I guess the roller coaster of this year hasn’t ended after all.
