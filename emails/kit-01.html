Hello, and welcome to the first Keepintouch! To start this whole thing off, I wanted to answer a question that some of you may have — why a newsletter? Of all forms of communication, why choose the slow one, the one that won’t pop up in your messages, the one that is essentially one-sided?

To answer this, I’d like to return us to somewhere around 2012. I was nine. A close friend of mine had just moved to North Carolina, and we wanted to keep in touch. I didn’t have a phone, so we wrote each other letters. This was incredibly exciting for nine-year-old me. Maybe it was the fact that I liked to write, or that no one could stop me from drawing pictures in the margins (as my teachers had done on my notes). Maybe it was the way it had felt when my mom came in with the mail and held up the letters, or the way the paper felt like it had traveled many miles. Whatever it was, the excitement of letter writing remained with me.

I wrote a few more letters in the intervening years. I had a few other pen-pals. I have had less and less chances to write letters recently, mainly because letter writing takes a while! Even if you’re typing, letters are tough. They can’t be answered in seconds like texts, and you don’t get a chance to rephrase or correct your mistakes. Every word has to be intentional. While this makes them harder to write, this also makes the experience of reading a letter more meaningful. These aren’t words I sent you hurriedly with The Pitt on in the background, they are words I plucked from the dark in a quiet room. (Granted, I still encourage you to write your newsletters with The Pitt on in the background if that decision is intentional.) This significance makes letters the perfect way to share small pieces of our expansive lives (as Brian would say, diamonds) because it necessitates reflection, or the coming to of some kind of point. Whether we read about your aunt’s new dog or your morning run, we will come away having learned something interesting about your life and possibly about our own.

On the topic of learning, we can move back to the first part of “newsletter” — news. It comes as no shock that I want to touch on news considering the hours I whittled away at the backrooms of the Alligator office, but I promise I’m not about to diatribe on the Alachua county school board elections. News, at its simplest, is just information about current events. Why shouldn’t we include within those all-important events the lives of our friends? A communal newsletter allows us to prioritize each other’s special moments on the same level as the next judicial nominee. At a time when the news bears down on us with its wide-reaching despair, I hope these fun little stories will return our attention to the people we care about.

A little bit about how this process will work:

  • Every Friday, you’ll receive a Keepintouch newsletter written by one of us.
  • There is a group sign up-sheet (linked below) to sign up to write a week’s newsletter.
    • You can write about anything, it just has to relate at least a little bit to your life and be less than 800 words.
    • If you don’t know where to start, check out the prompts list in the sign up sheet.
  • You’ll submit your newsletter through the submission form(also linked below) by Wednesday night.
  • If you’re not writing for a week, you can still submit questions, answers to previous week’s questions, or other news with the same form.
    • Questions can be, again, anything — from roommate problems to career advice.
    • You’ll also have the opportunity to answer last week’s questions.
    • Other news is just anything short you’d like to share, like “Rebekah got a cat” or “Del found this typography resource.”

With that, I’d like to announce that Keepintouch signups are now open! I hope that you will choose to tell us a story from your life. I see a beautiful future where we never hesitate to visit each other’s cities or ask each other for advice, but that horizon can only arrive if we all walk toward it.

Please Keepintouch :)
