Hello everyone,

Firstly, I wanted to start by saying how wonderful it is that you have this way of staying connected with one another. We often talk about college being all about the people you meet and this really exemplifies that. Secondly, thanks for letting me crash it and write to you all. It's an honor.

It has actually been a strange few weeks for me as Olivia and the girls have been away in France for three whole weeks. Because of this, it has just been me and our dogs (Iggy and Greta) in little ol' Gainesville. This strange lack of responsibility has led me to do three main things: listen to music a lot louder than usual, watch "weird youtube" (not that weird), and thinking a lot about teaching design. So I thought I'd use these as the structure of my email today, and in typical David fashion, this comes with a lot of links. You know I love a link.

Weird Youtube I'll start by addressing this up front. I don't mean that weird. What I mean is that, whenever Olivia is away, I often find myself watching after effects tutorials. This is...kinda weird. So when presented with three weeks of watching whatever I wanted, I found some interesting stuff.

  • Listers: A Glimpse Into Extreme Birdwatching A two hour film about the world of birdwatching in the US that had me in creases.
  • Fred again: Behind the Sound. An excellently crafted film with some amazing storytelling.
  • The Best Motion Design & Animation of 2026 (So Far...). Some brilliant examples of human-made art with some awe-inspiring process work.

Now, as a parent my general perspective of Youtube is: don't look directly at it, it will slowly steal your soul and/or brain. But this adult time with youtube was a nice reminder that there are still people out there making cool things. Some of it is overwhelming, but I was up for being whelmed.

Listen to music a lot One of the things that happens when you have kids is you have to think differently about the kind of music you listen to. There is a joke about hipster parents that we won't subject ourselves or our kids to anthropomorphic animal music, but we inevitably do. So when I found myself driving around Gainesville I thought, 'I don't have to listen to Caspar Babypants today!'. After watching the Fred Again video I shared above, I realized I could listen to this quite loud whenever I wanted. That was the inspiration behind by my song choice for today's note, but as I am cheating, here are some more songs that stuck with me on this last few weeks.

  • Faith Folorunso - King Lear
  • Pastor T.L. Barrett & The Youth For Christ Choir - Like a Ship (Without a Sail)
  • Your Brother's Keeper - Mantra

Thinking a lot about teaching design. This semester I'm teaching Mint & Visual Systems. The hard and brilliant work that you all did in these classes has really helped me shape my understanding of how to teach them. Thank you. The thing I am really wrestling with right now is the not-so-small elephant in the room which is; post-graduate job market chaos. This isn't just hitting designers, this instability is hitting everyone. What does it mean to be in higher education when it's promise is being so radically challenged? I don't have an easy answer for this, and I'm sure you all are wrestling with it's reality in a much more challenging way than I am in the comfort of my position. One thing I have settled on for now, is the importance of being in-person with one another. Higher education still gives people with different views the chance to share space together and think about what it means to be in the world. This is important! When we think about that, design education also becomes a lot less about learning how to use specific software or typefaces, and more of a focus on our experiences as people and how we can bring those into our work. So as I have spent the last three weeks overworking, I also want to say:

  • You are not your job
  • Design is not everything
  • People (and the planet) are the point

Which, is why something like keepintouch is so brilliant.
